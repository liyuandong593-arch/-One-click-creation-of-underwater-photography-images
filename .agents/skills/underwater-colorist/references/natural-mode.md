# Natural Mode

Natural mode is the baseline grading mode for underwater images.

Its purpose is to produce a result that feels professionally corrected, believable, clean, transparent and restrained.

Natural mode should preserve the original atmosphere of the dive.

It is not intended to create dramatic cinematic effects.

---

## 1. Core Goal

Natural mode should improve:

- white balance
- subject readability
- water cleanliness
- midtone clarity
- credible color recovery
- depth separation

while preserving:

- underwater atmosphere
- original composition
- original lighting
- realistic haze
- realistic color loss
- natural depth

The final image should feel like:

“A very well-exposed and professionally corrected underwater photograph.”

---

## 2. Priority Order

Natural mode follows this priority:

1. preserve original content
2. preserve original light logic
3. improve primary-subject readability
4. clean water color
5. improve midtone separation
6. recover credible warm color
7. refine local contrast
8. apply restrained sharpening

Style should never override realism.

---

## 3. Water Color

Natural mode does not aim for maximum blue saturation.

First determine whether the water contains:

- natural blue
- cyan contamination
- green contamination
- gray haze
- excessive blue saturation

### Preferred result

Water should look:

- clean
- transparent
- believable
- slightly restrained
- spatially layered

### Avoid

- electric blue
- swimming-pool blue
- dirty green
- cyan wash
- gray water
- fake tropical color

---

## 4. White Balance

White balance should correct obvious underwater color contamination without removing the underwater feeling.

### Goals

- reduce dirty green
- reduce excessive cyan
- preserve natural coolness
- keep neutral equipment believable
- prevent skin from becoming orange

### Rule

Do not force the image toward land-photography white balance.

Underwater scenes may remain naturally cool.

---

## 5. Exposure

Use exposure conservatively.

Natural mode should not make every region bright.

### Priorities

- face
- person
- important marine life
- important coral
- primary structure

### Rule

Fix local subject exposure before applying large global exposure changes.

Background depth should remain believable.

---

## 6. Midtones

Midtone correction is one of the most important parts of Natural mode.

Many underwater images feel dark because midtones are muddy, not because blacks are too deep.

### Improve

- subject/background separation
- body readability
- fish visibility
- reef detail
- mid-water clarity

### Avoid

- lifting all shadows
- flattening contrast
- making deep-water scenes look shallow

---

## 7. Shadows

Shadows may remain dark.

Natural mode should preserve natural underwater contrast.

### Rule

Dark areas should contain useful information when they are important.

Do not force all shadows to become visible.

### Avoid

- crushed primary subjects
- lifted gray blacks
- flat underwater depth

---

## 8. Highlights

Protect:

- water surface
- bubbles
- reflective fish
- white diving equipment
- artificial-light hotspots

Highlights may remain bright but should preserve transition and texture.

Avoid clipped white regions whenever possible.

---

## 9. Haze

Natural underwater haze is part of the scene.

Do not remove it completely.

### Preferred approach

Reduce:

- dirty haze
- gray veil
- excessive top-water fog

Preserve:

- atmospheric depth
- distance falloff
- softer distant subjects

Clear water does not mean zero haze.

---

## 10. Person Skin

Skin should remain believable within an underwater environment.

### Goals

- reduce cyan contamination
- reduce green contamination
- restore moderate warmth
- preserve natural texture

### Avoid

- orange skin
- red skin
- yellow skin
- magenta skin
- plastic skin
- land-studio skin tone

When uncertain, slightly cool and believable is preferred over aggressively warm.

---

## 11. Fish

Fish correction should remain conservative.

Preferred order:

1. exposure
2. tone
3. micro-contrast
4. restrained clarity
5. minimal semantic enhancement only if necessary

### Hard Rules

- do not generate fish
- do not change direction
- do not change count
- do not redraw body geometry

Distant fish should naturally remain softer than near fish.

---

## 12. Coral and Reef

Natural mode should recover reef color without creating an aquarium look.

### Preferred treatment

- preserve cool water
- recover restrained brown / orange / red reef tones
- protect pale coral
- maintain realistic saturation

### Avoid

- universal orange shift
- universal yellow shift
- excessive red recovery
- highly saturated reef colors unsupported by the source

---

## 13. Shallow Water

Shallow natural-light images often need less correction.

### Priorities

- preserve surface light
- maintain clean water
- correct mild cyan / green contamination
- improve subject readability
- avoid over-processing

Rule:

If the original source is already strong, do less.

---

## 14. Deep Water

Deep-water Natural mode must preserve depth.

### Preferred result

- darker water
- readable primary subject
- controlled midtones
- restrained warm recovery
- realistic artificial light

### Avoid

- tropical shallow-water appearance
- invented sun rays
- globally lifted blacks
- overly bright backgrounds

Darker but clearer is often correct.

---

## 15. Artificial Light

Respect the original light source.

Do not invent new light.

Artificial light should:

- remain directional
- decay with distance
- preserve surrounding darkness
- avoid large flat white areas

---

## 16. Sharpening

Sharpen selectively.

Priority:

- primary subject
- face
- important equipment
- near fish
- important reef detail
- structural edges

Keep:

- distant water
- distant fish
- haze

softer.

Avoid global aggressive sharpening.

---

## 17. Noise

Underwater shadow recovery often reveals noise.

Preferred sequence:

1. noise reduction
2. tonal correction
3. restrained local sharpening

Do not sharpen noisy shadow regions aggressively.

---

## 18. Natural Mode QC

Before approval, confirm:

### Content

- composition unchanged
- no new fish
- no new coral
- no new marine life
- no anatomy changes
- no equipment changes

### Water

- water is cleaner
- no excessive blue saturation
- no dirty green cast
- depth still feels underwater
- haze remains believable

### Subject

- primary subject is readable
- person skin is credible
- fish geometry is intact
- important reef detail is visible

### Tone

- midtones improved
- highlights controlled
- important shadows retain information
- background still has depth

### Texture

- no excessive sharpening
- no plastic skin
- no artificial AI detail
- no obvious halos

---

## 19. Natural Mode Pass Condition

Natural mode passes when:

- the image looks cleaner and more readable
- the scene still feels like the original dive
- color recovery is believable
- the user does not notice the grading before noticing the photograph

Natural mode should feel invisible.

That is its success condition.
